    Audio service — for the links a browser cannot read

    There is nothing to set up here. No page served from here can read YouTube, SoundCloud or Bandcamp audio, on any browser — it sits behind short-lived signed links their hosts share with nobody — so this site runs a small service that can, and uses it for you. No account, no key, nothing to sign into. It only answers this site and it limits how much any one connection may ask of it, so it stays up for everybody.

    It is ripper/ in this site’s repository: one file of Node around yt-dlp, a Dockerfile and a README. Run your own and put its address here — that wins over the built-in one, is kept in this browser only, and is never sent anywhere but to the address you typed.

    Ticking that sends nothing anywhere. Links a browser can read for itself still work; the rest fall back to recording a tab, where the browser allows it.

    Paste it and press Fetch, or Enter, and a WAV is saved as soon as the audio lands — the clip stays in the list if you want to chop it first. A direct file comes straight off the link where the host shares its files; a .pls or .m3u is followed to the stream behind it and taken from until you press Stop; and anything a browser may not read for itself — YouTube, SoundCloud, Bandcamp — goes to the audio service below, which is the only thing that works on a phone and takes seconds rather than real time.

    For something already playing somewhere. Pick a tab in the picker and share its audio; it stops itself once the sound ends. Chrome and Edge only.
    ClipSaves asAudio

    Drag across the waveform to take a section, drag either edge to nudge it, click once to drop it. Space plays what is selected — or the whole clip if nothing is. Save selection writes out just that part, named after where it starts, with whatever is ticked under Output applied. Take as many as you like; the clip stays whole. The picture is scaled to fill the box, so a quiet recording is still readable — it says view ×n when it has done that, and nothing about the audio itself is changed.

    2 · Output

    Everything is off to begin with on purpose: by default you get the audio that was in the clip, untouched. Leave as decoded is not the rate it was recorded at — the browser resamples everything to its own audio rate on the way in, and that rate is what comes back out.

    2 · Output

    How this works. An SX/A card holds plain WAVs, so they are read straight off it and the pad each one sits on comes from PAD_INFO.BIN. The MKII keeps its samples in internal memory, so export them to the card from the unit first (EXPORT SAMPLE) and this tool tidies up what it wrote. Left on “keep original” with nothing ticked, the audio is copied through sample for sample — only the Roland header is dropped and the file renamed.
